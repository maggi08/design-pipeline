# Interview question bank

`/design-brief` asks from here. A round is at most four questions through the structured question tool, each with options and a recommended one first; "Other" is always there. Ask what changes the design; skip what the materials already answer; never ask a design-jargon question the user cannot answer ("which type scale ratio?") — show options instead.

## Round 1 — the order (every project)

- Who is the client and what do they sell or run? (one line; from the materials if present)
- What must this project change for them? Options: more leads / sales; less manual work; a clear picture of money or operations; a launch or an event; replacing an old site or tool.
- Who uses it? Options: their customers; their staff; the owner alone; partners or teachers or couriers (name the role).
- What is in scope? Options (multi): a landing page or site; a web app (CRM, admin, dashboard); a mobile app; an online shop.

## Round 2 — by surface

### Persuade (landing, site, shop front)
- The one action a visitor must take: buy, book, leave a phone number, sign up, call.
- What makes the client different from the three nearest competitors (ask for names or links).
- What proof exists: reviews they can show, numbers, logos, photos of real work, certificates. Nothing invented: missing proof is an open question, not a placeholder.
- Sections the client already imagines; then propose the ones they did not (FAQ, pricing, process, guarantees) and mark them as proposals.
- Tone: options from the business (calm and expert, warm and local, bold and young, premium and quiet).

### Operate (CRM, admin, dashboard, back office)
- The three jobs done most often, in the user's words ("add a lesson", "see who owes money").
- Entities and how they relate (students, groups, teachers, payments) — draw a one-line chain.
- The numbers someone looks at every day or every month; the moment of truth (month-end, a shift, a delivery).
- Roles and what each must not see.
- What they use today (Excel, a notebook, an old system) and what hurts in it.
- Devices: desktop daily, phone for quick checks, a shared tablet.

### Mobile
- Online all the time or must it work offline?
- What happens with one thumb on the go vs sitting down?
- Notifications: which events, how often.

## Round 3 — constraints (every project)

- Languages of the interface (and of the content). Kazakh, Russian and other Cyrillic content changes font choice.
- Brand assets: logo, colours, fonts, photos — or none.
- References the client likes or hates (links, screenshots). A liked reference is a mood, not a template to copy.
- Deadline and the first version's scope: what can wait for v2.
- Target stack if fixed (Next.js + shadcn, Nuxt, a CMS) — it decides the token names and the component kit.

## Brainstorm

After the rounds, propose two or three concepts in plain words, each one paragraph: the angle, the main screen, what the client gets. Add a short list of features or sections nobody asked for that this kind of business usually needs. The user picks, merges or rejects; only what they accept goes into BRIEF.md, marked as accepted proposals.

## What goes into BRIEF.md

Facts the user stated, accepted proposals (marked), and open questions. A guess is never written as a fact. A number or a name you invented for a mockup is marked as an example.
